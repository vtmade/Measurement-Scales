# Glossary of Terms

## A

**Alpha (α)** - See Cronbach's Alpha

**Average Variance Extracted (AVE)** - A measure of the average amount of variance that a latent construct explains in its observed indicators (items). AVE > 0.50 indicates that more than half of the variance in items is explained by the construct. Used to assess convergent validity in CFA.

## B

**Bartlett's Test of Sphericity** - A statistical test used before factor analysis to check if correlations among variables are large enough to conduct factor analysis. A significant result (p < .05) indicates factor analysis is appropriate.

**Base Rate** - The prevalence or frequency of a condition/characteristic in a population. Important for interpreting sensitivity and specificity of diagnostic scales.

## C

**CFA (Confirmatory Factor Analysis)** - A statistical technique used to test whether a hypothesized factor structure fits the observed data. Used to confirm the factor structure identified through EFA.

**CFI (Comparative Fit Index)** - A model fit index in CFA ranging from 0 to 1. Values ≥ .95 indicate good fit, .90-.95 indicate acceptable fit.

**Classical Test Theory (CTT)** - A psychometric theory that posits observed scores = true scores + error. Foundation for reliability concepts.

**Cohen's Kappa (κ)** - A statistic measuring inter-rater agreement for categorical ratings, correcting for chance agreement. Ranges from -1 to +1.

**Communality** - The proportion of variance in an observed variable that is explained by the extracted factors. In factor analysis, values > .40 are typically desired.

**Composite Reliability (CR)** - A measure of internal consistency calculated from CFA factor loadings. Interpreted similarly to Cronbach's alpha. Often more accurate as it doesn't assume equal item loadings.

**Concurrent Validity** - A type of criterion validity where the scale and criterion are measured at the same time.

**Construct** - An abstract concept or theoretical variable (e.g., intelligence, satisfaction, anxiety) that cannot be directly observed but is inferred from observable indicators.

**Construct Validity** - The extent to which a scale actually measures the theoretical construct it claims to measure. Encompasses factorial, convergent, discriminant, and nomological validity.

**Content Validity** - The extent to which scale items comprehensively and relevantly represent the construct domain. Typically assessed through expert judgment.

**Content Validity Index (CVI)** - Quantitative measure of content validity based on expert ratings. I-CVI (item-level) and S-CVI (scale-level) are commonly reported.

**Content Validity Ratio (CVR)** - A statistic (developed by Lawshe) measuring agreement among experts about item essentiality. Ranges from -1 to +1.

**Convergent Validity** - Evidence that a scale correlates with other measures of the same or related constructs. A type of construct validity.

**Criterion** - An external standard or outcome against which a scale is validated (e.g., diagnosis, performance, behavior).

**Criterion Validity** - The extent to which a scale correlates with or predicts an external criterion. Includes concurrent and predictive validity.

**Cronbach's Alpha (α)** - The most commonly used measure of internal consistency reliability. Ranges from 0 to 1, with ≥ .70 generally acceptable and ≥ .80 preferred.

**Cross-Loading** - When an item loads substantially (typically > .30) on more than one factor in factor analysis. Generally undesirable.

## D

**Dimensionality** - The number of distinct constructs or factors measured by a scale. Scales can be unidimensional (one factor) or multidimensional (multiple factors).

**Discriminant Validity** - Evidence that a scale does NOT correlate highly with measures of theoretically distinct constructs. Demonstrates uniqueness of the measure.

## E

**EFA (Exploratory Factor Analysis)** - A statistical technique used to discover the underlying factor structure of a set of variables. Used when structure is unknown or uncertain.

**Eigenvalue** - In factor analysis, represents the amount of variance explained by a factor. The Kaiser criterion suggests retaining factors with eigenvalues > 1.0.

**Error Variance** - The portion of variance in observed scores attributable to random error rather than the true score.

## F

**Face Validity** - The extent to which items appear to measure what they claim to measure, based on superficial inspection. Weakest form of validity evidence.

**Factor** - A latent (unobserved) variable that explains correlations among a set of observed variables (items).

**Factorial Validity** - Evidence that items cluster together (load on factors) in ways consistent with the theoretical structure. A type of construct validity.

**Factor Loading** - The correlation between an item and a factor. Indicates how strongly an item relates to the underlying factor. Loadings > .50 are typically desired.

**False Negative** - A case incorrectly classified as negative (e.g., missing a true case of depression).

**False Positive** - A case incorrectly classified as positive (e.g., identifying depression when not present).

**Fornell-Larcker Criterion** - A test of discriminant validity comparing the square root of AVE to correlations with other constructs. √AVE should exceed inter-construct correlations.

## G

**Gold Standard** - An established, widely-accepted measure or diagnostic procedure against which new measures are validated.

## H

**Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT)** - A measure of discriminant validity comparing between-trait to within-trait correlations. Values < .85 (conservative: < .70) support discriminant validity.

## I

**Incremental Validity** - The extent to which a scale explains variance in a criterion beyond what is explained by other measures.

**Internal Consistency** - The degree to which items within a scale correlate with each other and measure the same underlying construct. Measured by Cronbach's alpha, omega, or composite reliability.

**Inter-Item Correlation** - The correlation between two items in a scale. Average inter-item correlations of .30-.70 are typically desired.

**Inter-Rater Reliability** - The consistency of scores when multiple raters independently rate the same targets. Measured by ICC or Cohen's kappa.

**Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC)** - A measure of reliability for continuous ratings from multiple raters or across time points. Various forms (ICC(1,1), ICC(2,1), etc.) for different designs.

**Item** - A single question, statement, or indicator in a scale.

**Item-Total Correlation** - The correlation between an item and the total scale score (with that item removed). Values > .40 indicate good items.

## K

**Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) Measure** - A statistic assessing sampling adequacy for factor analysis. Values > .80 are good, > .90 are excellent.

**Kaiser Criterion** - A rule for determining number of factors to retain: keep factors with eigenvalues > 1.0. Should be used with other methods (scree plot, parallel analysis).

## L

**Latent Variable** - An unobserved construct measured indirectly through observed indicators (items). Same as factor in factor analysis.

**Likert Scale** - A common response format where respondents indicate level of agreement (e.g., 1=Strongly Disagree to 5=Strongly Agree).

## M

**Maximum Likelihood (ML)** - A common estimation method in factor analysis and SEM. Assumes multivariate normality.

**McDonald's Omega (ω)** - A measure of internal consistency reliability that doesn't assume equal item loadings (unlike alpha). Often more accurate than Cronbach's alpha.

**Measurement Error** - The difference between an observed score and the true score. Random error affects reliability; systematic error affects validity.

**Measurement Invariance** - The extent to which a scale measures the same construct in the same way across groups (e.g., cultures, time). Necessary for meaningful group comparisons.

**Modification Indices (MI)** - In CFA, statistics suggesting changes that would improve model fit. Should only be applied if theoretically justified.

## N

**Nomological Network** - The network of theoretical relationships among constructs. Includes antecedents, consequences, and correlates of a construct.

**Nomological Validity** - Evidence that a scale behaves according to theoretical expectations within its nomological network.

## O

**Oblique Rotation** - Factor rotation method that allows factors to correlate. Common methods: Promax, Oblimin. Typically more realistic for psychological constructs.

**Orthogonal Rotation** - Factor rotation method that constrains factors to be uncorrelated. Common method: Varimax.

## P

**Parallel Analysis** - A method for determining number of factors to retain by comparing eigenvalues from actual data to those from random data.

**Parallel Forms Reliability** - Consistency between two equivalent forms of a measure. Relevant when multiple test versions exist.

**Predictive Validity** - A type of criterion validity where the scale measured at Time 1 predicts a criterion measured at Time 2 (future).

**Principal Axis Factoring (PAF)** - A common extraction method in exploratory factor analysis. Analyzes shared variance only.

**Principal Components Analysis (PCA)** - A data reduction technique often confused with EFA. Analyzes all variance (not just shared variance).

**Promax Rotation** - A type of oblique factor rotation that allows factors to correlate.

## R

**Reliability** - The consistency or stability of measurement. A reliable measure produces similar scores under consistent conditions.

**Response Format** - The type of response options provided (e.g., Likert scale, yes/no, frequency rating).

**RMSEA (Root Mean Square Error of Approximation)** - A model fit index in CFA. Values < .06 indicate good fit, .06-.08 acceptable.

**ROC Curve (Receiver Operating Characteristic)** - A plot of sensitivity vs. (1-specificity) across different cut-scores. Used to evaluate diagnostic accuracy.

## S

**Scale** - A set of items designed to measure a construct. Can be unidimensional or multidimensional.

**Scree Plot** - A graph of eigenvalues used to determine number of factors. Look for "elbow" where slope flattens.

**Sensitivity** - In diagnostic scales, the proportion of true cases correctly identified (true positive rate).

**Skewness** - The degree of asymmetry in a distribution. Values between -2 and +2 are typically acceptable for factor analysis.

**Specificity** - In diagnostic scales, the proportion of true non-cases correctly identified (true negative rate).

**SRMR (Standardized Root Mean Square Residual)** - A model fit index in CFA. Values < .08 indicate good fit.

**Standard Error of Measurement (SEM)** - The average amount of error in individual scores. Used to construct confidence intervals around scores.

**Subscale** - A subset of items within a larger scale measuring a specific dimension or facet.

## T

**Test-Retest Reliability** - The stability of scores when the same measure is administered to the same people at two time points. Measured by correlation between time points.

**TLI (Tucker-Lewis Index)** - A model fit index in CFA. Values ≥ .95 indicate good fit, .90-.95 acceptable.

**True Score** - In classical test theory, the actual (error-free) value of what is being measured. Never directly observable.

## U

**Unidimensional** - A scale measuring a single construct or factor. All items load on one factor.

## V

**Validity** - The extent to which a scale measures what it claims to measure. Encompasses content, construct, and criterion validity.

**Varimax Rotation** - A type of orthogonal factor rotation that constrains factors to be uncorrelated.

**Variance Extracted** - The proportion of variance in items explained by the factor. See Average Variance Extracted (AVE).
