# Finding & Reusing Validated Scales

## Overview

**Most of the time, you should use an existing validated scale rather than create a new one.** This guide shows you how to find, evaluate, and properly reuse validated measurement scales.

---

## Why Reuse Existing Validated Scales?

### Benefits of Using Existing Scales

✅ **Save time and resources** - Skip 1-3 years of validation work
✅ **Build on proven measures** - Use scales with established reliability and validity
✅ **Enable comparison** - Compare your findings with other studies
✅ **Strengthen credibility** - Use accepted measures in your field
✅ **Support meta-analysis** - Contribute to cumulative knowledge
✅ **Avoid reinventing the wheel** - Researchers have already done the hard work

### When You Should Use an Existing Scale

**Use an existing scale when:**
- ✅ A validated measure exists for your construct
- ✅ The scale fits your population and context
- ✅ Psychometric properties are strong (α > .70, good validity evidence)
- ✅ The scale is accessible (free or affordable)
- ✅ You want to build on prior research
- ✅ You have limited resources for validation

### When You Might Need a New Scale

**Consider developing a new scale only when:**
- ⚠️ No adequate measure exists for your specific construct
- ⚠️ Existing scales don't fit your population (age, culture, language)
- ⚠️ All existing scales have serious psychometric flaws
- ⚠️ Theory has significantly evolved
- ⚠️ You have substantial resources for proper validation

**Rule of thumb:** Search thoroughly for existing scales first. Creating a new scale should be a last resort.

## Public Databases of Validated Scales

### Free & Publicly Accessible Databases

#### 1. **PsycTESTS (APA)** ⭐ MOST COMPREHENSIVE
- **What:** 20,000+ psychological tests and measures
- **Access:** Requires institutional subscription (check with your library)
- **Coverage:** All areas of psychology
- **Features:**
  - Full-text measures when available
  - Detailed psychometric information
  - Citation information
- **URL:** https://www.apa.org/pubs/databases/psyctests
- **How to access:**
  - If affiliated with university: Access through library
  - If not: Ask librarian for interlibrary loan or document delivery

#### 2. **ETS Test Collection** ⭐ FREE ACCESS
- **What:** 25,000+ tests and research instruments
- **Access:** FREE - No subscription needed
- **Coverage:** Education, psychology, aptitude
- **Features:**
  - Searchable database
  - Test descriptions and citations
  - Some full-text measures
- **URL:** https://www.ets.org/test-link/about
- **Best for:** Educational assessments, cognitive tests

#### 3. **PROMIS (Patient-Reported Outcomes)** ⭐ FREE ACCESS
- **What:** Health-related measurement tools
- **Access:** FREE - Publicly available
- **Coverage:** Physical, mental, and social health
- **Features:**
  - Item banks and short forms
  - All measures freely available
  - Scoring guides included
  - Multiple languages
- **URL:** https://www.healthmeasures.net/explore-measurement-systems/promis
- **Best for:** Health outcomes research

#### 4. **NIH Toolbox** ⭐ FREE ACCESS
- **What:** Neurological and behavioral assessments
- **Access:** FREE for research (registration required)
- **Coverage:** Cognition, emotion, motor, sensation
- **URL:** https://www.healthmeasures.net/explore-measurement-systems/nih-toolbox
- **Best for:** Neuropsychological research

#### 5. **COSMIN Database of Systematic Reviews** ⭐ FREE ACCESS
- **What:** Reviews of measurement properties for health instruments
- **Access:** FREE
- **Coverage:** Health-related scales with quality ratings
- **URL:** https://database.cosmin.nl
- **Best for:** Finding high-quality health measures

### Domain-Specific Free Resources

#### **Organizational/Workplace**
- **SIOP Resources**
  - URL: https://www.siop.org
  - Best for: Job attitudes, organizational behavior
  - Note: Some measures in published articles

#### **Mental Health**
- **NIMH Measures & Materials**
  - URL: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/research-domain-criteria-rdoc
  - Best for: Psychopathology, mental disorders

#### **Personality**
- **IPIP (International Personality Item Pool)** ⭐ COMPLETELY FREE
  - URL: https://ipip.ori.org
  - What: Public-domain personality items
  - Features: Alternatives to copyrighted scales
  - License: No permission needed, freely usable

#### **Quality of Life**
- **WHOQOL (World Health Organization)**
  - URL: https://www.who.int/tools/whoqol
  - Access: FREE
  - Languages: 40+ languages

## Searching for Scales by Topic

### Step-by-Step Search Strategy

#### Step 1: Start with Google Scholar
**Search terms:**
```
"[construct name]" scale validation
"[construct name]" measure psychometric
"[construct name]" questionnaire reliability validity
```

**Examples:**
- "job satisfaction" scale validation
- "emotional intelligence" measure psychometric
- "resilience" questionnaire reliability validity

**What to look for:**
- Highly cited papers (100+ citations)
- Recent validation studies (last 10 years)
- Published in reputable journals

#### Step 2: Check PubMed (for health-related)
**Search terms:**
```
[construct][Mesh] AND (scale OR questionnaire OR instrument) AND validation
```

**Example:**
- depression[Mesh] AND scale AND validation

#### Step 3: Search PsycINFO (via library)
**Use subject headings:**
- "Test Construction" AND "[your construct]"
- "Test Validity" AND "[your construct]"

#### Step 4: Check Scale Databases
Search in:
- PsycTESTS
- ETS Test Collection
- PROMIS (for health)
- COSMIN (for health)

## Evaluating Whether a Scale is Good

### Checklist: Is This Scale Reliable and Valid?

Before using a scale, check these criteria:

#### ✅ **Reliability**
- [ ] Cronbach's α ≥ .70 (acceptable) or ≥ .80 (good)
- [ ] Test-retest reliability reported (if appropriate)
- [ ] Reliability demonstrated in multiple samples

#### ✅ **Validity Evidence**
- [ ] Content validity established (expert review)
- [ ] Construct validity demonstrated:
  - [ ] Factor structure tested (EFA and/or CFA)
  - [ ] Convergent validity (correlates with similar measures)
  - [ ] Discriminant validity (doesn't correlate with unrelated measures)
- [ ] Criterion validity (predicts relevant outcomes)

#### ✅ **Sample & Population**
- [ ] Validated with sample similar to yours
- [ ] Sample size adequate (N > 200 for factor analysis)
- [ ] Diverse samples tested (if applicable)

#### ✅ **Practical Considerations**
- [ ] Appropriate length (not too long for your context)
- [ ] Reading level fits your population
- [ ] Available in your language (or validated translation)
- [ ] Accessible (free or affordable)

#### ✅ **Publication Quality**
- [ ] Published in peer-reviewed journal
- [ ] Multiple validation studies exist
- [ ] Used by other researchers (check citations)
- [ ] Authors are established researchers

### Red Flags (Avoid These Scales)

🚩 **Only one validation study** with small sample (N < 100)
🚩 **Cronbach's α < .70** consistently
🚩 **No factor analysis** reported
🚩 **Only convergent validity** (no discriminant validity)
🚩 **Published only in predatory journals**
🚩 **No independent replications**
🚩 **Authors cannot provide psychometric information**

## How to Properly Cite and Use Scales

### Citation Guidelines

#### **When Using an Existing Scale in Your Work:**

**In your method section, cite:**
1. **The original validation study**
2. **Key psychometric properties**
3. **Sample items (if allowed by copyright)**
4. **Your sample's reliability**

**Example:**
> "Job satisfaction was measured using the Brief Index of Affective Job
> Satisfaction (Thompson & Phua, 2012), a 4-item scale assessing emotional
> reactions to one's job. Items include 'I feel enthusiastic about my job'
> rated on a 5-point scale (1 = Never to 5 = Extremely often/Always). The
> original validation demonstrated good internal consistency (α = .85-.87)
> and convergent validity with longer satisfaction measures (r = .76-.81).
> In our sample, Cronbach's alpha was .83."

#### **Citation Format (APA 7th):**

**Original scale development:**
```
Spector, P. E. (1985). Measurement of human service staff satisfaction:
  Development of the Job Satisfaction Survey. American Journal of
  Community Psychology, 13(6), 693-713.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00929796
```

**Scale from database:**
```
Rosenberg, M. (1965). Rosenberg self-esteem scale (RSE). Acceptance and
  commitment therapy. Measures Package, 61(52), 18.
```

### Attribution Requirements

Different scales have different requirements:

#### **Public Domain Scales (No Permission Needed)**
- Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale
- Most IPIP scales
- Many scales from 1960s-1970s

**What to do:** Cite original source, use freely

#### **Freely Available for Research (Cite and Use)**
- PANAS
- Satisfaction with Life Scale
- PHQ-9, GAD-7
- Most academic scales in journal appendices

**What to do:**
- Cite original source
- Include scale in appendix (if journal allows)
- Some authors request notification (courtesy)

#### **Copyrighted/Commercial (Permission Required)**
- NEO-PI-R (commercial)
- MMPI (commercial)
- Maslach Burnout Inventory (commercial)

**What to do:**
- Contact publisher for permission
- Pay licensing fees (if applicable)
- Follow usage restrictions

## Copyright and Permissions

### Do I Need Permission?

#### **You DON'T need permission if:**
✅ Scale is in public domain
✅ Scale is published in journal appendix and freely available
✅ Authors explicitly state "free for research use"
✅ Using for academic/research purposes (fair use may apply)

#### **You DO need permission if:**
⚠️ Scale is commercially published (sold by test publisher)
⚠️ You're using for commercial purposes
⚠️ You want to modify the scale
⚠️ You're translating the scale
⚠️ Copyright notice says "all rights reserved"
⚠️ You're using for clinical decisions (some scales)

### How to Request Permission

**Email template:**

```
Subject: Permission to Use [Scale Name] for Research

Dear Dr. [Author],

I am writing to request permission to use the [Scale Name] in my
research project titled "[Your Project Title]."

Project details:
- Purpose: [Academic research / Thesis / Dissertation / etc.]
- Population: [Who you're studying]
- Sample size: Approximately [N] participants
- Context: [Brief description]
- Timeline: [When you'll collect data]

The scale will be used solely for research purposes and will not be
used commercially. I will properly cite the original validation study:
[Full citation]

Could you please confirm if I may use this scale? If there are any
requirements, fees, or conditions, please let me know.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Affiliation]
[Email]
```

**Response timeline:** Allow 2-4 weeks

### Free vs. Paid Scales

#### **Free Scales (Research Use)**
Most academic scales are free for research. Examples:
- Scales published in journal articles
- Scales in PsycTESTS marked "available"
- PROMIS measures
- IPIP scales
- Government-funded scales (NIH, WHO)

#### **Paid/Commercial Scales**
Some scales require purchase. Examples:
- NEO-PI-R: ~$300-400 for kit
- MMPI: ~$200+ per use
- Maslach Burnout Inventory: ~$150+
- Myers-Briggs Type Indicator: Licensed use only

**Alternatives:** Look for free public-domain alternatives
- Instead of NEO-PI-R → Use IPIP-NEO (free)
- Instead of MBI → Use Copenhagen Burnout Inventory (free)

## Step-by-Step: Finding a Scale for Your Needs

### Example Walkthrough: Finding a "Resilience" Scale

#### Step 1: Google Scholar Search
**Search:** "resilience scale validation"

**Top results:**
- Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC)
- Brief Resilience Scale (BRS)
- Resilience Scale for Adults (RSA)

#### Step 2: Evaluate Each Scale

**Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC)**
- Items: 25 (original), 10 (short form)
- Reliability: α = .89-.94
- Validation: Multiple samples, good evidence
- Cost: Copyrighted, requires permission
- Citation: Connor & Davidson (2003)

**Brief Resilience Scale (BRS)**
- Items: 6
- Reliability: α = .80-.91
- Validation: Good psychometrics
- Cost: FREE for research
- Citation: Smith et al. (2008)

**Decision:** Use BRS (free, brief, well-validated)

#### Step 3: Access the Scale

**Option 1:** Find in original publication
- Search for "Smith et al. (2008) Brief Resilience Scale"
- Check if scale is in article appendix

**Option 2:** Check PsycTESTS
- Search database
- Download if available

**Option 3:** Email authors
- If not publicly available, request from authors

#### Step 4: Verify It Fits Your Needs

**Check:**
- ✅ Items make sense for your population
- ✅ Reading level appropriate
- ✅ Length feasible
- ✅ Psychometrics acceptable
- ✅ Validated in similar context

#### Step 5: Use and Cite Properly

**In your study:**
1. Administer scale as designed (don't modify without validation)
2. Calculate reliability in your sample
3. Cite original validation paper
4. Report your sample's psychometrics

## When to Adapt vs. Use As-Is

### Use As-Is When:
✅ Population matches original validation
✅ Context is similar
✅ Language is the same
✅ Scale works for your purposes

**Just check reliability in your sample**

### Minor Adaptation When:
⚠️ Need to update outdated terms
⚠️ Change "employees" to "students" (context shift)
⚠️ Adjust reading level slightly

**Required:**
- Justify changes
- Check reliability and factor structure
- Report modifications

### Major Adaptation When:
⚠️ Translating to new language
⚠️ Using with very different population
⚠️ Significantly different context

**Required:**
- Full validation process
- Translation/back-translation
- New reliability and validity evidence

**See:** [Best Practices - Adapting Scales](09-best-practices.md#adapting-existing-scales)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### **Q: Can I use a scale I found in a journal article?**
A: Usually yes for research purposes. Cite the article and use the scale as published. If in doubt, contact the authors.

### **Q: Do I have to pay to use most scales?**
A: No. Most academic research scales are free for research use. Commercial scales (NEO-PI-R, MMPI, etc.) require purchase.

### **Q: Can I modify items to fit my context better?**
A: Minor wording changes are okay (e.g., "employees" → "students"), but report this. Major changes require revalidation.

### **Q: What if the scale I want is copyrighted?**
A: Contact the copyright holder for permission. Sometimes there are free alternatives (e.g., IPIP versions of personality scales).

### **Q: How do I know if a scale is good enough?**
A: Check: (1) α ≥ .70, (2) validated in multiple samples, (3) published in peer-reviewed journal, (4) good factor analysis results.

### **Q: The scale I want has 50 items. Can I use just 10?**
A: Only if a validated short form exists. Don't create your own short version without validation.

### **Q: Can I combine items from different scales?**
A: Not recommended. Use validated scales as designed. Combining creates a new unvalidated measure.

### **Q: What if no scale exists for my construct?**
A: First, search thoroughly (see search strategies above). If truly nothing exists, you may need to develop a new scale. See [Validation Timeline](06-validation-timeline.md).

### **Q: Is it okay to use a scale from a dissertation?**
A: Use caution. Check if it's been peer-reviewed or published elsewhere. Dissertations have less rigorous review than journals.

### **Q: How do I handle multiple language versions?**
A: Use validated translations when available. Check PROMIS and other databases for translated versions. Don't use Google Translate.

## Quick Reference Checklist

Before using a scale, complete this checklist:

**Finding the Scale:**
- [ ] Searched scale databases (PsycTESTS, ETS, PROMIS)
- [ ] Searched Google Scholar and PubMed
- [ ] Found original validation paper
- [ ] Checked for more recent validation studies

**Evaluating the Scale:**
- [ ] Reliability ≥ .70 in published studies
- [ ] Factor analysis conducted (EFA/CFA)
- [ ] Validity evidence presented (convergent, discriminant)
- [ ] Sample similar to mine
- [ ] Appropriate length

**Copyright & Access:**
- [ ] Determined if free or paid
- [ ] Located full scale (article, database, or author)
- [ ] Obtained permission if needed
- [ ] Understand usage restrictions

**Using the Scale:**
- [ ] Will use scale as designed (no modifications)
- [ ] Will cite original validation study
- [ ] Will calculate reliability in my sample
- [ ] Will report psychometrics properly
